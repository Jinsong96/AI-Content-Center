City birds may be changing their songs so that they can be heard over traffic noise. A team of researchers recorded the songs of great tits in ten European cities over three years. They compared the recordings with the amount of traffic noise at each place. The team found that birds in noisy areas sang at a higher pitch than birds in quiet areas. Higher sounds do not travel as far as low sounds, but they are easier to hear when the background is loud. The scientists think the birds are not born with this habit. Young birds learn their songs from older birds nearby. So when the older birds sing higher, the young birds copy them, and over time the whole local song changes. The researchers also found that the change is not always good for the birds. A higher song may be harder for female birds to understand. The team says the study is a clear example of how human activity can change animal behaviour in a short time. Some cities have started to build low walls along roads to block the noise, and the walls may help the birds sing in their older, lower way.
