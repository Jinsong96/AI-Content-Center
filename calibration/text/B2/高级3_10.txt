Puzzling About the Platypus

Scientists like to make sense of the animal world. To help them do that, they classify animals into groups. All the animals in each group have the same characteristics. Scientists noticed that many animals share these four characteristics: • They have hair or fur. • They breathe air. • They do not lay eggs. Instead, they give birth to babies. • They nurse their babies. Scientists decided that all animals having these characteristics would be called mammals. Humans, bears, whales, kangaroos, and dogs are mammals. All the scientists agreed. Then, a little more than 200 years ago, they made a

discovery. It was the platypus, a creature never seen before. It lived in the creeks and ponds and lakes of Australia. What was it? The scientists wanted to classify it. So they looked at its characteristics. It had a big bill, like a bird. It had webbed feet, like a bird. It even laid eggs, like a bird. But it did not have wings. Could it be a weird kind of bird without wings? Maybe. But it had fur, like a mammal. It breathed air, like a mammal. And it nursed its babies, like a mammal. Was it a weird kind of mammal that laid eggs? The scientists decided. Yes, it was a weird kind of mammal. And that is how the platypus broke the rules and changed our ideas about what a mammal can be.
